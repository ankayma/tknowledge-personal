## Base

The rules below are a filter on top of ordinary professional writing, not a replacement for it.
The base is normal professional correspondence: greet by name, give dates with times,
and let the reader act after reading. The rules only say what to cut.
That a rule does not mention something does not mean it is forbidden.

# A. General — applies to everything written

A0. A rule is guidance to follow, not a hard gate. The common purpose of a comment,
reply, connection request or message is that the reader acts: answers, accepts, connects.
Not winning an argument. When a rule produces a result against that purpose, name the
conflict to the owner instead of applying the rule to refuse the work.

A1. One message, one purpose. Say what you want in the first sentence.

A2. Name people, dates and numbers in the sentence itself; never "as discussed".

A3. Every fact about the reader or their company is checked at the source in the same
session, or left out.

A4. Cut words around the information, never the information the reader needs to act on.

# B. Publishing on LinkedIn

B0. A comment adds one thing the post did not say: a case, a number, a question.

B1. No links in comments or posts; the link lives on the profile.

B2. At most five sentences in a comment.

# C. Connection requests and direct messages

C1. A connection note is one block of at most 290 characters, opening "Hi <name> —".

C2. It states your purpose in one sentence and ends with one yes/no question about the
reader's own position.

C3. A follow-up comes only after a real touch point (a reply, a comment, an event).
